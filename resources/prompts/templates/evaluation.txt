You are a professional story editor and quality evaluator. Evaluate the following story draft across four quality levels using a detailed rubric.

DRAFT TEXT:
---
{draft_text}
---

EVALUATION CONTEXT:
- Language: {language}
- Target length: {target_words} words
- Original topic: {original_topic}
- Intended tone: {tone}
- Intended perspective: {perspective}
- Voiceover requirements: {voiceover_requirements}

EVALUATION RUBRIC:

LEVEL 1 — TECHNICAL (mechanical correctness):
- Word count: Is the draft within 10% of the target ({target_words} words)?
- Markers: Are there any leftover [Chapter X], [Part X], [Section X] markers?
- AI artifacts: Are there any meta-comments (Note:, Author's note:), AI disclaimers, or "The End" markers?
- Stage directions: Are there any bracketed stage directions, sound effects, or non-narrative text?
- Encoding: Is the text clean (no garbled characters, no BOM, no mojibake)?
- Language consistency: Is the entire text in {language} with no unintended code-switching?

LEVEL 2 — LINGUISTIC (language quality):
- Grammar: Is the grammar correct throughout?
- Naturalness: Does the prose read naturally in {language}, or does it feel machine-translated?
- Vocabulary: Is the vocabulary appropriate for the register and audience?
- Sentence variety: Is there good variation in sentence length and structure?
- Idiom usage: Are idioms and expressions natural and culturally appropriate?
- Readability: Is the text easy to follow without re-reading?

LEVEL 3 — CONTENT (storytelling quality):
- Topic adherence: Does the story faithfully address the original topic "{original_topic}"?
- Plot completeness: Does the story have a clear beginning, middle, and end?
- Character development: Are characters well-developed with clear arcs?
- Consistency: Are there any plot holes, contradictions, or continuity errors?
- Engagement: Is the story compelling and likely to hold a listener's attention?
- Pacing: Does the story maintain appropriate pacing without dragging or rushing?
- Tone: Does the story maintain the intended tone of "{tone}" throughout?
- Perspective: Is the perspective "{perspective}" consistent throughout?
- Hook quality: Does the story open with an effective hook that creates immediate curiosity or tension within the first 3–5 sentences? (Score 0 if the story opens with weather, backstory, or generic scene-setting.)
- Retention techniques: Does the story use audience retention devices such as: open loops (hinting at revelations before delivering them), delayed revelations (building tension before showing content), somatic responses (specific physical reactions after shocks instead of abstract "I felt terrible"), sensory anchors (recurring sensory details that gain meaning), echo phrases (callbacks to earlier moments), or breather scenes (quiet moments between high-intensity beats)? Count how many distinct techniques are used — score higher for 6+ techniques applied effectively.
- Emotional concreteness: Does the story show emotions through specific physical sensations and actions rather than telling through abstract statements? Flag any instances of cliché emotional shortcuts ("my heart sank", "a chill ran down my spine", "I could not believe my eyes").
- Antagonist depth: Does the antagonist (if present) have clear motivation, a coherent plan, and dimensionality — or are they a flat villain?
- Protagonist transformation: Does the protagonist change meaningfully by the end, or do they remain static?

LEVEL 4 — VOICEOVER (TTS/narration readiness):
- Sentence clarity: Are sentences simple and clear enough for comfortable narration?
- Pause structure: Does punctuation create natural breathing points for a narrator?
- Pronunciation: Are there words that would be difficult for TTS systems or human narrators?
- Flow: Does the text flow smoothly when read aloud, without awkward constructions?
- Headers/markers: Are there any remaining headers or markers that should not be read aloud?
- Dialog attribution: If dialog is present, is it clearly attributed and easy to follow aurally?
- Dialog craft: Are dialog scenes written as vivid exchanges with speech tags, physical actions, and internal reactions between lines — or are they bare back-and-forth without narrative tissue?
- Paragraph length: Are there single-sentence paragraphs or excessively long paragraphs that would disrupt narration rhythm? (Ideal: 2–5 sentences per paragraph.)

SCORING:
- Score each level from 0.0 to 10.0 (one decimal place).
- 9.0+ = Publication ready. 7.0–8.9 = Good with minor issues. 5.0–6.9 = Needs revision. Below 5.0 = Major rewrite needed.
- The overall_score is the weighted average: L1 (15%), L2 (25%), L3 (35%), L4 (25%).
