DIALOG CRAFT — MAKE CONVERSATIONS VIVID:
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Dialogs must be SCENES, not summaries. Never write "We discussed it." Write the actual exchange.

Structure of a dialog beat:
[Setting/tension — 1–2 sentences: where are they, what is the atmosphere]
"Spoken line," [speech tag + physical action/gesture].
[Internal reaction — 1–3 sentences: the protagonist's thoughts, feelings, memories triggered by what was just said]
"Response," [speech tag + body language/tone].
[Pause/silence/detail — 1–2 sentences: a beat of silence, exchanged glances, a background detail]
"Next line," [tag + action].

Speech tags — VARY THEM. Do not repeat the same tag within 5 lines:
- Neutral: said, asked, replied, answered
- Intensity: whispered, hissed, snapped, growled, muttered
- Emotion: stammered, choked out, managed to say, forced out
- Manner: said flatly, said with deliberate calm, said through clenched teeth

Physical actions during speech — show the body, not just the voice:
- Pressing lips together, clenching a jaw until the muscle shows
- Folding arms, turning away to the window, drumming fingers
- Adjusting hair nervously, twisting a ring, gripping the edge of a table
- Standing up slowly, leaning back, stepping closer

Between dialog lines, ALWAYS include at least one of: internal thought, physical reaction, sensory detail, or silence beat. Never stack three or more bare dialog lines without narrative tissue between them.

VOICEOVER FORMAT:
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

- Write in clean prose paragraphs. No bullet points, no numbered lists.
- Use simple, clear sentences that sound natural when read aloud. Average sentence length: 12–20 words. Mix short punchy sentences (5–8 words) with longer flowing ones (20–25 words) for rhythm.
- Use punctuation to create natural pauses: commas for brief pauses, periods for full stops, ellipses (…) for dramatic pauses, em dashes (—) for interruptions or asides.

- Dialog must be clearly attributed before or after the spoken line — a listener needs to know who is speaking before they hear the words.
